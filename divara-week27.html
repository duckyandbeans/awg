Adverb Usage Examples
Expresses a characteristic or evaluates an action
(can be placed before or after the verb)		 They slowly walked along the beach.
They walked slowly along the beach.
They walked along the beach slowly.
Indicates the time of an action
(usually placed at the beginning or end of a sentence)		 We'll arrive at the hotel soon.
We'll soon arrive at the hotel.
Soon we'll arrive at the hotel.
Shows how often an action occurs
(can be placed before or after the verb)		 She rarely travels by train.
She travels by train rarely.
Indicates the degree or extent of something
(usually placed before the word it defines)		 We were really tired by the end of the journey.
She drives very fast!

⚠️ Watch Out!

The word friendly ends in -ly, but it is an adjective, not an adverb. To make an adverb from this word, we use the phrase in a friendly way. Some adverbs, like fast, hard, early, do not have a single-root adjective.

⚠️ Watch out!

In the fixed phrase at home, a possessive pronoun is never used:

  • Jenny is not at home today. (Correct)
  • Jenny is not at her home today. (Incorrect)

💡 Helpful hints

The choice of preposition used with the word hospital depends on what is meant: in hospital (as a patient), at the hospital (as a visitor or employee):

  • Kelly is in hospital with pneumonia.
  • Philip is at the hospital visiting his mother.

⚠️ Watch out!

In fixed phrases like go home and come home, a preposition indicating place/direction or the preposition to is never used:

  • I came home at two o'clock. (Correct)
  • I came to my home at two o'clock. (Incorrect)
  • I came to home at two o'clock. (Incorrect)

💡 Helpful hints

The preposition to is usually used with verbs of movement (come, go, move, run, walk, drive, etc.) to indicate movement from one place to another:

  • She drove to Berlin last summer.
  • I'm walking to school this morning.

The preposition in is used to indicate finding an object inside some space:

  • She lives in Berlin.
  • The travel agent is in Oxford.
