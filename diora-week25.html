Inversions & Possessives

Negative Adverbial InversionWhen a sentence starts with a negative or restrictive adverb (Never, Seldom, Rarely, Hardly, Not only), the subject and auxiliary verb are inverted.
Ex: Not only did she win, but she also broke the record.
Possessives (Double Genitive)We use "of + possessive pronoun" or "of + noun's" to specify possession among a larger group.
Ex: A friend of mine (not 'a friend of me'). A colleague of my father's.

Conditional Sentences

ZeroIf + Present, Present. (Facts, scientific truths)
FirstIf + Present, Will + V1. (Real, possible future situations)
SecondIf + Past, Would + V1. (Unreal, hypothetical present/future)
ThirdIf + Past Perfect, Would have + V3. (Unreal past, regrets)
MixedIf + Past Perfect, Would + V1. (Past condition with a present result)

Relative Clauses

Who / WhomFor people. (Who = Subject, Whom = Object)
Which / ThatFor things or animals. ('That' is only used in defining clauses).
WhoseFor possession. (The girl whose bag was stolen...)
Where / WhenFor places and times.
